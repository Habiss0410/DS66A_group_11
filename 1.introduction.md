# Marketing Campaign Response Prediction
### Predicting Customer Response to a Portuguese Bank's Telephone Marketing Campaigns

---

## 1. Introduction

A Portuguese bank ran **direct marketing campaigns by phone** to persuade customers to subscribe to a **term deposit**. In this product, the customer places money with the bank for a fixed period and earns a fixed interest rate, which gives the bank a stable source of funding.

Phone outreach is expensive. Reaching a decision often took more than one call to the same customer, and most customers who were contacted did not subscribe: only about 12% said "yes".

> **Research question:** Using only what the bank knows about a customer before dialing, can we predict who is likely to subscribe, so that calls can be focused on the most promising customers?

This project answers that question with machine learning. The practical aim is to move from **calling everyone** to **prioritizing the right customers**, reducing wasted calls and raising the conversion rate.

---

## 2. Problem Definition

This is a **binary classification** problem. From a customer's profile, financial status and contact history, the model predicts whether the customer will:

- **subscribe** to the term deposit, or
- **not subscribe** to the term deposit.

### Target Variable

| | |
|---|---|
| **Variable** | `y` (encoded as 1 / 0 after cleaning) |
| **Values** | `yes` (subscribed) / `no` (did not subscribe) |
| **Distribution** | ~11.7% yes, ~88.3% no *(imbalanced)* |

Because the classes are imbalanced, accuracy alone is misleading: predicting "no" for every customer would already be about 88% accurate. Models are therefore compared mainly by **PR-AUC** and **ROC-AUC**, with Precision, Recall and F1-score reported at a decision threshold chosen on training data.

### Dataset

The data is the **Bank Marketing** dataset (`bank-full.csv`): **45,211 observations** and **17 variables** (16 inputs and 1 target). Records are ordered by date. After data cleaning, **45,208 observations** remain (3 rows with a call duration of 0 were removed), with no missing values. The 16 inputs fall into four business groups:

| Group | Variables | Key question |
|---|---|---|
| Customer profile | `age`, `job`, `marital`, `education` | Who is the customer? |
| Financial status | `balance`, `housing`, `loan`, `default` | What is their financial situation? |
| Current campaign | `contact`, `day`, `month`, `duration`, `campaign` | How and when was the customer contacted? |
| Previous campaign | `pdays`, `previous`, `poutcome` | How did the customer respond before? |

### Important modeling decisions

- **`duration` is excluded from the main models.** Call duration is only known after the call ends, so it cannot be used to decide whom to call. It is kept only in an appendix to show how much it would inflate the results.
- **`day` is dropped** because it carries little business meaning, and `pdays = -1` ("never contacted before") is converted into a separate indicator, `was_contacted_before`.
- **Evaluation uses two splits:** a stratified random 80/20 split, and a time-based split (train on the earlier 80% of records, test on the latest 20%) because the data is ordered by date.

---

## 3. Project Objectives

This project uses Python for data analysis and machine learning to:

**🔍 Identify influencing factors**
Examine which customer-profile, financial and campaign-history factors are associated with a customer's decision to subscribe, using feature importance from each model.

**🤖 Build and compare predictive models**
Develop three classifiers (Logistic Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost), tune them with Optuna using cross-validated PR-AUC, and select the best model on training data only, keeping the test set for final reporting.

**📈 Create business value**
Turn predicted probabilities into an actionable call list. By choosing a decision threshold, the bank can balance reaching more potential subscribers against making fewer unproductive calls, and so shift from **mass phone calling** to **targeted marketing**.
